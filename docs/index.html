What the scanner finds

A starting map for your engineers and whoever reviews your DPDP readiness. Every finding shows its evidence, a confidence level, why it matters and what to do.

Personal data in your code

Emails, phone numbers, Aadhaar, PAN, addresses, dates of birth, card, bank and UPI details, health and children's data, found in schemas, models, forms and APIs.

Who receives it

About 50 services that usually get personal data: analytics, ads, payments, SMS and email, support chat, AI providers and more, found from your dependencies and code.

Risky patterns

Secrets committed to the repo, personal data written to logs, and real people's details hardcoded in source code.

Your website

Trackers loaded by your homepage, cookies set before consent, and a keyword check of your privacy notice for key DPDP elements.

Runs where your code already is

On a laptop in seconds, or on every pull request with the GitHub Action. A 36,000-file codebase scanned in about 11 seconds in our tests.

Run it locally or in CI

No account, no upload, no runtime dependencies.

Read the report

Markdown, HTML, JSON, or SARIF that GitHub shows inline in pull requests.

Fix and re-run

Fail the build on high-severity findings so new leaks don't ship.

Private by design

A privacy tool should not create new privacy risks.

  • The scanner runs on your machine or your CI runner. No code, data or results are sent to us.
  • Open source under Apache-2.0, so you can read exactly what it does.
  • Secrets and personal data are masked in every report.
  • This website uses no cookies, no analytics and no third-party scripts.

Questions

Is this legal advice or a compliance certificate?

No. DPDP Ready shows engineering facts: where personal data appears in your code and which services receive it. Whether you comply with the DPDP Act depends on your purposes, consent, contracts and processes, and should be reviewed by a qualified professional.

Do you see my code?

Not when you use the scanner: it runs entirely on your machine or CI. For an assessment, you can run it yourself and share only the report, or give us temporary read-only access that you revoke afterwards. Either way, we delete any copy of your code when the assessment ends.

What is the DPDP Act?

India's Digital Personal Data Protection Act, 2023. Together with the DPDP Rules, 2025, it sets out how businesses must collect, use, protect and delete personal data. Most obligations for businesses apply from 13 May 2027.

Which languages and frameworks does it support?

JavaScript and TypeScript (including React, Next.js and Node), Python, Java, Kotlin, Go, PHP, Ruby, Dart, SQL and Prisma schemas, plus common dependency files. Detection is pattern-based, so it can miss unusual field names and flag some fields that aren't personal data. Each finding shows its confidence.

Is the scanner really free?

Yes. It's open source under Apache-2.0, including commercial use. The paid part is the hands-on assessment and, later, a hosted dashboard for teams.
